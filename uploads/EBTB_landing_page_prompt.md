# EBTB Landing Page Prompt

## Brand & Product Context

**Product**: EBTB — everything-bagel-inspired seasoning blend (and/or salad) launching in Hungary. The acronym *EBTB* is the brand identity; underneath, in small supporting text, it expands to: *E*verything, *B*ut, *T*he, *B*agel.

**Market Position**: First-mover in Hungary's early-stage everything-bagel seasoning category. No established competitors—only small artisanal players. This is a category-definition moment: we want EBTB to become *the* reference brand for this flavor profile.

**Audience**: Health-conscious, food-curious Hungarians. Early adopters who shop eMAG, gourmet/health retailers, and appreciate accessible luxury in their kitchen. Age range likely 25–50, urban-leaning, familiar with international food trends but rooted in Hungarian culinary traditions (túró, rántotta, fresh salads).

**Go-to-Market**: DTC + eMAG first, then health/gourmet retail, supermarkets, and dm.

---

## Design Direction

### Subject & Thesis
Open with the sensory, unmistakable character of everything bagel: the visible spice blend, the flavor complexity, the "you can put this on *everything*" versatility. The hero should make someone who's never seen everything bagel stop and ask "what *is* that?" — it's visually distinctive, tactile, almost edible through the screen.

### Visual Identity

**Palette** (4–6 named hex values):
- **Charcoal base** (#2B2B2B or #1F1F1F): Grounded, premium, lets the product (and spices) shine.
- **Cream/Off-white** (#F9F8F6): Warm, approachable; food-friendly background.
- **Rust/Terracotta** (#C85A3A or similar): The color of toasted sesame, poppy seed, and dried garlic. This is the signature accent—it echoes the blend's visual texture.
- **Deep green** (#4A5D4A or earthy forest): Optional accent for freshness, especially if positioning as salad/healthy. Grounds the palette.
- **Gold/Amber** (#D4A76A): Subtle highlight for "golden" qualities—warmth, richness, premium feel.

Avoid warm cream + terracotta defaults by grounding the palette in charcoal and letting rust be a *hero* accent, not just a warm tone.

**Typography**:
- **Display face** (hero, main headline): A bold, geometric sans-serif or a confident serif with personality. Examples: Raleway (bold), Poppins (bold), or a custom serif like Freight Display or Sentinel. The typeface should feel intentional and a little unexpected—not Helvetica.
- **Body face** (copy, CTAs, smaller text): A readable, warm humanist sans-serif like Inter, Outfit, or DM Sans. Clean, modern, but not sterile.
- **Utility** (captions, labels, metadata): A monospace or compact utility face for ingredient lists, details, or data.

**Type Scale & Hierarchy**:
- Hero headline: Large, bold, personality-forward. Emphasize *EBTB* as the acronym; the expansion (*Everything, But, The, Bagel*) lives underneath in smaller supporting text, rendered in a lighter weight or alternate style.
- Section headlines: 2.5–3rem, bold, in the display face.
- Body text: 1rem–1.125rem, comfortable line-height (1.6–1.8), generous letter-spacing to feel open and premium.
- All-caps labels and eyebrows: Sparingly, and only when they encode real structure (e.g., "How it works," "What's inside").

## Page Structure & Content

### Hero Section
**Visual**: Close-up, high-resolution shot (or illustration) of the EBTB spice blend—sesame seeds, poppy seeds, garlic, onion, salt, and any other visible components. Make it tactile and craveable. OR: A hand sprinkling EBTB over a beautifully plated dish (e.g., avocado toast, a salad, soft cheese and túró).

**Headline**: 
> **EBTB**
> 
> *Everything. But. The. Bagel.*

Subheading (copy-forward):
> The seasoning for everything—salads, breakfast, cheese, roasted vegetables, dips. Hungary's first everything-bagel blend. First-to-market. First-to-your-table.

**CTA**: "Shop now" (link to eMAG or DTC storefront) + secondary "Learn more" (scroll down).

**Design Note**: Let the hero image dominate 60–70% of the viewport on desktop. The text should be placed with breathing room, possibly overlaid on a solid or semi-transparent background for legibility. On mobile, stack: image full-width, then text overlay or below.

---

### Section 2: What Is EBTB? (The Pitch)

**Headline**: Everything-bagel, explained.

**Content**:
- Short paragraph: What is everything bagel? A beloved US seasoning trend—sesame, poppy, garlic, onion, salt, and a little chili flake. Savory, umami-forward, addictive. Now in Hungary.
- Subheading: "Why EBTB?"
  - No translation needed—it's a global flavor.
  - No established brand in Hungary—EBTB is the reference.
  - Craveable on everything: salads, cheeses, avocado, rántotta, vegetables, spreads.

**Visual**: 2–3 hero product shots (e.g., the jar/packet at different angles) or lifestyle shots (sprinkling on real food). Avoid overly styled stock photography; aim for authentic, slightly imperfect, food-blogger energy.

**Design Note**: Keep copy concise and benefit-forward. Use short sentences. Structure with implied hierarchy—do not number or over-formalize.

---

### Section 3: How to Use It (Lifestyle/Versatility)

**Headline**: Where does it go?

**Content**: 4–5 brief use cases, each with:
- A small, distinctive illustration or food photo
- A one-liner: e.g., "On your morning rántotta," "In your next salad," "With fresh túró and fruit," "Over roasted vegetables," "As a dip seasoning"

**Design Note**: This section should feel fresh and playful. Consider a grid layout (2 columns on desktop, 1 on mobile) or a horizontal scroll on mobile. Let the variety of use cases speak to the product's versatility without over-explaining.

---

### Section 4: What's Inside (Transparency)

**Headline**: The blend.

**Content**: A simple, clean breakdown of the ingredients:
- Sesame seeds
- Poppy seeds
- Garlic
- Onion
- Sea salt
- Chili flake
- (Any other key ingredients)

**Design Note**: Consider displaying this as:
- A simple ingredient list (prose or bullet format)
- A visual "flavor wheel" or spice breakdown (small illustration showing the components)
- A minimal table or labeled section

Avoid overly technical jargon; this is about transparency and appetite appeal.

---

### Section 5: Why First-to-Market Matters

**Headline**: Category leadership starts now.

**Copy** (1–2 sentences):
> We're not copying a trend—we're defining the category in Hungary. First-mover advantage means you get authentic, intentional flavoring from day one. No compromises.

**Design Note**: This section can be minimal and confidence-forward. A single paragraph, maybe with a supporting quote or statement. This is a moment for restraint; let the copy do the work.

---

### Section 6: Shop Now (CTA Section)

**Headline**: Ready to taste it?

**Content**:
- Primary CTA: "Buy on eMAG" (link to eMAG storefront)
- Secondary CTA: "DTC Store" or "Coming Soon" (link to own storefront if live, or waitlist)
- Tertiary: "Follow for updates" (social media link or email signup for when it's available elsewhere)

**Design Note**: Make the primary CTA visually distinct (rust/terracotta background, high contrast). Secondary CTA can be outlined or text-link style. Generous padding, clear hover states.

---

### Section 7: Footer

**Content**:
- Brand tagline or mission statement (1–2 sentences)
- Social links (Instagram, maybe TikTok for recipe content)
- Email signup for early access / recipe updates
- Legal: Terms, Privacy Policy (if applicable), possibly a note on availability ("Currently shipping to Hungary; EU expansion coming")

**Design Note**: Keep footer clean and functional. Avoid cluttering with unnecessary links.

## Signature Design Element

**The Acronym Expansion**: This is the memorable detail. The hero headline should treat "EBTB" as the primary mark, with the expansion (*Everything. But. The. Bagel.*) as a supporting detail—smaller, lighter weight, possibly in a different color or with subtle animation (e.g., a subtle fade-in on scroll or page load). This detail should appear consistently across the site, reinforcing brand recognition.

Alternatively (or additionally): Use the visible spice texture as a recurring visual motif—perhaps a subtle background pattern, a divider element, or an accent that repeats across sections. This roots the design in the product's tactile, real-world character.

## Technical Requirements

- **Responsive**: Mobile-first. Test down to 375px width.
- **Performance**: Optimize all images (WebP format where possible). Aim for fast load times—this is a food/lifestyle site, so visuals matter, but speed matters more.
- **Accessibility**: 
  - Clear focus states on all interactive elements.
  - Sufficient color contrast (WCAG AA minimum).
  - Alt text on all product and lifestyle images.
  - Semantic HTML.
- **Motion**: 
  - Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
  - Use animation deliberately (e.g., hero image fade-in, section reveals on scroll). Avoid overstimulation.
- **Interactivity**:
  - Hover states on links and CTAs (color shift, underline, or background fill).
  - Active states clearly visible.
  - No keyboard traps; all CTAs accessible via Tab.

- **Conversational, confident, not hype-y**: We're not screaming; we're inviting.
- **Benefit-forward**: Focus on what you *do* with it, not features.
- **Playful but grounded**: Acknowledge the product is a trend, but own it with authenticity.
- **Approachable**: English and Hungarian-friendly; no insider jargon.

## Success Criteria

1. **First impression**: A visitor lands on the page and immediately understands: "This is a premium, intentional food product. It's new to Hungary, but it's not a copy—it's the real thing."
2. **Conversion**: Clear path to purchase (eMAG link prominent and clickable).
3. **Memorability**: The EBTB acronym expansion is visually distinct and sticks in memory.
4. **Mobile experience**: Equally polished on phone and desktop.
5. **Shareability**: The page looks great in a screenshot or social share (for when customers post about it).

---

## Notes for the Builder

- This is *not* a blog or portfolio—keep it focused and tight. One clear job: sell the product and position it as category-defining.
- Assume the audience is familiar with international food trends but may not know "everything bagel" in detail—educate gently in Section 2.
- Don't oversell; let the product and the market opportunity speak.
- Photography/imagery is critical. If using stock, source high-quality food and lifestyle photography that feels intentional, not generic.
- Consider adding a faint background texture or subtle animation to avoid feeling sterile, but keep it minimal—let the product and content be the star.
